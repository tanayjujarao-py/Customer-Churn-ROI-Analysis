# QUESTION 2: WHY ARE THEY LEAVING?

## ROOT CAUSE ANALYSIS:

### 1\. SUPPORT QUALITY ISSUE:

* ## 0 tickets: 6% churn

* ## 1-2 tickets: 24% churn

* ## 3-5 tickets: 74.1% churn

* ## 6+ tickets: 97.6% churn   **Finding:** More support contact \= higher churn (quality problem)

* ## Fast resolution (\<24hr): 19.2% churn

* ## Slow resolution (48hr+): 82.1% churn   **Finding:** Slow support resolution drives 4x higher churn

### 2\. ENGAGEMENT/ACTIVATION ISSUE:

* ## High feature usage (70+): 22.2% churn

* ## Low feature usage (\<40): 61.7% churn   **Finding:** Low engagement customers churn at 2.7x rate

* ## High email engagement: 29.3% churn

* ## Low email engagement: 52.3% churn   **Finding:** Disengaged customers are 2x more likely to churn

### 3\. CONVERSION/ORDERING ISSUE:

* ## Never ordered: 67.1% churn (635 customers)

* ## 1 order only: 59.3% churn

* ## 11+ orders: 2.3% churn   **Finding:** Customers who don't order or order once are high churn risk

* ## Ordered within 14 days: 36.8% churn

* ## Never ordered: 67.1% churn   **Finding:** Early activation is critical

### 4\. SATISFACTION ISSUE:

* ## NPS Promoters (9-10): 15.4% churn

* ## NPS Detractors (0-6): 52.2% churn   **Finding:** Unhappy customers are 3.1x more likely to churn

### 5\. STATED REASONS (from churned customers):

## **Top 3 reasons:**

## 

1. ## Poor support (15.3%)

2. ## Found competitor (15.2%)

3. ## Financial reasons (14.3%)

## KEY INSIGHT \- THE ROOT CAUSES:

## Customers churn because of THREE interconnected problems:

## 

1. ## 6+ support tickets

2. ## Slow resolution

3. ## Never ordered

## 

## The common thread: Lack of Value/Friction in the Customer Journey.

* **Cause of Churn:** Customers struggle to use the product effectively or fail to realize its benefit.  
* **Manifestations of Friction:**  
  * High support ticket volume (6+).  
  * Slow resolution.  
  * Citing "poor support."  
* **Manifestations of Lack of Value:**  
  * Low feature usage.  
  * Low email engagement.  
  * Failure to place orders (or only one order).  
  * Moving to a competitor.  
* **Insight:** Churn is often triggered early due to initial friction or a failure to quickly find the product's core value.
